As of Monday, June 22, 2026, there are over 2,000 active 287(g) agreements according to ICE’s program website. More than 1,700 unique agencies now participate — roughly 9.5% of all law enforcement agencies in the United States.1

What's the 287(g) program?

In just 16 months, over 1,500 local police departments, county sheriffs’ offices, state highway patrols, and other LEAs have joined the immigration enforcement partnership program. And it’s all managed by the ICE component subagency, Enforcement & Removal Operations (ERO), that’s also responsible for:

  • overseeing the immigration detention system2, which marked its 20th death of 2026 this week when 63-year old Felix Alcorta-Rodriguez passed away in Texas.

  • detainee custody transfer and removal operations, like the daily flights moving ICE detainees between different processing + detention facilities, and the other set of daily deportation flights.3

  • enforcement operations4, including community-based abductions, coordinated arrests with local police, and other ‘first mile’ intake activity for the deportation machine.

Here’s how I described the deportation machine’s basic flow recently:

Think of it as a funnel, where the larger top end represents the intake into the detention and deportation system — primarily through arrests and jail detainer requests. At the smaller bottom of the funnel, we have the ultimate goal of this machine as it is currently configured by Stephen Miller and Tom Homan: remove as many individuals as possible.

And we now have some (messy and incomplete) data showing how the program’s growth correlates with ICE’s broader arrest numbers since last January. As I wrote in that same piece above:

So, the overall composition in ICE arrests has shifted dramatically over the past year, with 287(g) related arrests representing nearly 10% [9.5%] of arrests in January and February 2026, up from less than 3% [2.9%] in early 2025.

With a new peak in both active agreements (2,033) and participating agencies (1,731), I figured we’d take the opportunity to review some recent updates to the report, all intended to help us understand how this program fits into ICE’s larger deportation machine.

Live, and Learn, Más

Interactive visual report aside, there’s a lot more to this program than just big picture numbers. Data visualization isn’t much help for explaining how the scope of enforcement authority differs between the three program models, for example.

That’s why I’m also working to expand the About the Report + Learn More sections of the landing page to:

  • answer more of the common questions on how the 287(g) program works;

  • give more background context on where the data comes from and how it’s used in my report;

As I work to expand these areas, I’m also going to add some simple infographics and diagrams to help explain complex concepts, compare program models, etc.
