It’s the day after Christmas, and our government is building concentration camps.

They’ll call them ‘mega centers’ — giant warehouses designed to hold thousands of detained immigrants across the United States. But they’re really just concentration camps.

After NBC News first reported on ICE’s intent to purchase these warehouses last month, new reporting confirms these plans have now evolved into active work. Last week, Bloomberg highlighted a new no-bid contract issued to Kpb Services LLC, for just under the $30 million threshold that requires federal contracts to include an open bidding process1. The contract description is straightforward:

“DUE DILIGENCE SERVICES AND CONCEPT DESIGN FOR PROCESSING CENTERS AND MEGA CENTERS THROUGHOUT THE UNITED STATES”

It’s the day after Christmas, and our government is hiring armed private contractors in Texas to speed up the deportation process. Part of the logistical backbone behind the 287(g) program’s expansion, these contractors will be dispatched from a new “National Coordination Center” to be built in the Nashville, TN area.

“ICE requires ground transportation services to support up to 254 Texas counties participating in the 287(g) program with transportation to 36 ICE locations.” – SAM.gov, Notice ID 70CDCR26RFI000009

According to the request published by ICE on December 23rd, contract requirements include a 30-minute response time “to arrival at the pickup location”.2

Why Texas? As mentioned in the Performance Work Statement (PWS):

Texas maintains one of the largest and most active 287(g) programs in the nation, with more than thirteen thousand (13,000) credentialed DIOs.3

But it’s not just Texas residents that will be impacted. Block Club Chicago published a great report this week on enforcement data from Operation Midway Blitz, including some useful insights on just how much movement is happening between ICE facilities. As shown in this Block Club map, people are routinely moved from ICE’s facility in suburban Broadview, IL to facilities in Texas, Louisiana, Missouri, etc.

It’s the day after Christmas, and a large portion of our legal immigration system has been unilaterally paused by the Trump administration over vague, irrational “security concerns.”

The H-1B visa program has been reworked almost completely, from how the lottery works to the fees paid by sponsoring employers. And a revised, expanded travel ban will soon impact people from 40+ countries.

The refugee program has been effectively suspended since January, with an all-time low refugee cap set at just 7,500 people. Priority has been given by the Trump administration to essentially make the program available solely to white Afrikaners from South Africa.

Meanwhile, tourists from many countries will soon have to share their social media accounts (and 5 years of history) with the government before they’re allowed to enter the U.S.
