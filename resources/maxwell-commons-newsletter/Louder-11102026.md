# Louder.

As of Tuesday, November 10th, **2,390** law enforcement agencies across 38 states, Guam, and the Northern Mariana Islands have an active 287(g) agreement, according to ICE’s [published list](https://www.ice.gov/identify-and-arrest/287g).[^1]

That includes the Ingram Police Department in Kerr County, Texas, which signed a Task Force Model agreement with ICE in October 2025. [Last Sunday](https://www.propublica.org/article/deportations-ingram-texas-287g-jaron-alexander), ProPublica and the New York Times Magazine published an absurd exploration into the damage inflicted by the agency on the 1,900 or so Ingram residents, in just 12 months since the agency joined the 287(g) program.

![Headline, hero image, and byline for article ‘A Texas Cop Signed on to Help ICE Deport His Neighbors. It Tore the Town Apart’ (ProPublica + NYT Magazine, October 25, 2026).](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/633c65b9c093a7536c7dee46/6ade0f117dae157855f8a5c1_Screenshot%202026-10-25%20at%209.15.33%E2%80%AFAM.avif)

It’s an incredible deep-dive into our broken immigration system, the cash cow behind the ever-growing deportation machine, and the families and communities torn apart as a result. As the ProPublica authors, **Francesca D’Annunzio** and **McKenzie Funk**, note in the story’s summary: 

> "within a year, cops would hand over the equivalent of 10% of the town.”

If you haven’t already, you really should spend some time with it this week. And I promise I’d suggest that even if I wasn’t cited. But now that you mention it…

And from the start, you get a sense of how much work went into this deep-dive investigation, which involved months of interviews with people in Texas and Mexico.

## Not So Sunny Outside Philly

Jumping from Texas to Pennsylvania, where Will Bunch from the The Philadelphia Inquirer has written a series of articles on the devastating impact of immigration enforcement and local police collaboration in the state. I first connected with Will this summer, shortly after he published this piece on the Mt. Holly Springs police department and the jarring results of its ongoing 287(g) partnership with ICE.

‘This quaint Pa. borough is a ‘speed trap’ nabbing immigrants for ICE’ by Will Bunch, The Philadelphia Inquirer (July 30, 2026)

“While high-profile, publicity-seeking operations like the ones that targeted Minnesota or Chicago are out, ICE is increasingly relying on its 287(g) partners to make arrests without attracting as much public outcry.”

Since connecting, I’ve had the chance to work with him on data and local maps for several of his fantastic deep-dive articles on the 287(g) program’s impact on central PA communities. On September 10th, he [published an investigation](https://www.inquirer.com/opinion/pennsylvania-immigration-arrests-287g-local-police-collaboration-20260910.html) into its growing hold on local agencies in Pennsylvania’s “coal country” in Schuylkill County:

> “Some 14 law enforcement agencies — the most of any of Pennsylvania’s 67 counties — have inked 287(g) pacts with ICE, launching a new era of beat cops making immigration arrests that has roiled daily life in Schuylkill County.”

![Screenshot of Philadelphia Inquirer banner nav, headline and subheading, hero image, and byline for Will Bunch article titled ‘In PA. coal country, local police join with ICE to bring arrests, fear’, published Sept. 10, 2026.](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/633c65b9c093a7536c7dee46/6aa599ac97ee19af5db1e8c3_Screenshot%202026-09-10%20at%204.37.19%E2%80%AFPM.png)


Bunch referenced the report again in his **_Inquirer_** newsletter the following week, including this particularly eye-opening stat on program-related arrests in Pennsylvania:

> “Indeed, 287(g) data guru Andrew Thrasher has reported that a whopping 70% of the immigrants arrested in Pennsylvania under the program have no criminal record or pending charges.”

## Más alto.

The 287(g) program report, and this newsletter’s coverage on program developments, were also referenced by two Spanish-language national news sources in mid-September.

While Will Bunch’s “287(g) data guru” moniker is quite the compliment, my favorite job title might be ‘investigador independiente’, as I was referred to by N+ Univision. Both Univision and Conexión Migrante referenced the report in articles on a new contract between ICE and GardaWorld, in which the giant security services firm will move thousands of ICE detainees across Texas and surrounding states on behalf of the agency and its 287(g) partner agencies.

> "Texas concentra más acuerdos 287(g) que cualquier otro estado. Un registro elaborado por el investigador independiente Andrew Thrasher, a partir de datos de ICE, contabiliza 514 acuerdos con 422 agencias policiales texanas, frente a 352 acuerdos en Florida." - **Univision** (September 12, 2026)[^2]

More on that contract from [Project Salt Box here](https://www.projectsaltbox.com/p/ice-awards-gardaworld-up-to-382-million?utm_source=substack&utm_campaign=post_embed&utm_medium=web&embedding_publication_id=4533653).[^3]

## York County, PA

In mid-October, Will Bunch again referenced the report in a detailed investigation into how York County, PA became “the most dangerous place for immigrants in the Eastern U.S.:

“The best statewide numbers, which come from the data project and were analyzed by 287(g) stats guru Andrew Thrasher, show that more than 31% of all 287(g) arrests made in Pennsylvania from the start of 2025 through early August took place in York County, even though it only makes up 3.6% of the state’s population.”
