# Authorization Process

We’ve learned of a growing number of agencies that appear to have joined the program either without proper authorization, or outside the scope of their official capacity.

In several cases, the 287(g) MOA appears to have been signed by the sheriff or police chief without going through the formal authorization process, as set by the jurisdiction’s governing body, like the county board of commissioners or the city/town council. 

---

> [!Note]
> * Consider how this authorization process may be subject to state administrative procedures (see [Wyoming, below](https://www.aclu-wy.org/news/public-records-requests-result-in-inconsistent-authorization-of-287g-across-wyoming/)).
> * The absence of a documented authorization process does not automatically make an agreement unlawful. This requires local and state-level research to confirm authorization and reporting requirements — consider how the jurisdiction typically manages binding intergovernmental agreements that obligates funds or assumes new liability.
> * States also distribute authority between sheriffs and county boards differently, with some sheriffs holding independent constitutional authority

## What we’ve learned

### Wyoming

After [suing Laramie County](https://www.aclu-wy.org/cases/juntos-et-al-v-kozak/), the **ACLU of Wyoming** filed records requests with the seven other counties holding active agreements and with the Wyoming Highway Patrol, seeking proof of governing-body approval and of compliance with the Wyoming Administrative Procedures Act. 

The responses, [published in July 2026](https://live-awp-wyoming.pantheonsite.io/app/uploads/2026/07/ORR-Responses.pdf), reveal:  
  * Four (Campbell, Hot Springs, Lincoln, and Natrona) counties couldn’t produce any  records to show the agreements were ever considered or approved by their commissioners.  
  * One (Carbon) county did sanction participation, after a 2025 presentation from its sheriff.  
  * One (Crook) county is relying on a 1998 resolution that granted the sheriff blanket authority to enter agreements with other law enforcement agencies.  
  * One (Sweetwater) county passed a resolution in early July 2026 that retroactively ratified every agreement its former sheriff had entered with ICE since 2020.

**ZERO** of the responding agencies were able to provide any record of state administrative rulemaking compliance.

More on the Laramie County Sheriff’s Office: [https\://www\.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/06/12/us/politics/ice-local-police-immigration-287g](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/06/12/us/politics/ice-local-police-immigration-287g)

### Montana

Four Gallatin County residents sued the county and sheriff in April 2026 over a 2020 agreement the commission never ratified. The county agreed to suspend 287(g) operations pending a ratification vote at a public hearing in July 2026\.

### Pennsylvania

In [Make the Road Pennsylvania v. Harran](https://www.aclupa.org/cases/make-the-road-pennsylvania-v-harran/), the ACLU of Pennsylvania and the Community Justice Project challenged a Bucks County agreement signed without commissioner authorization. The trial court ruled for the sheriff on standing, finding no harm from an agreement not yet in effect. The sheriff then lost reelection; his successor ended the agreement in January 2026\. The appeal continues in Superior Court, with America First Legal defending.

## What to ask for

* minutes, agendas, and any resolution of the governing body referencing the agreement, ICE, or immigration enforcement, for a window around the signature date; 

* any legal opinion from the county attorney or outside counsel on the agency signatory’s authority to sign; 

* any filing made under the state administrative procedure act (if applicable);

* any blanket or standing delegation of this intergovernmental contracting authority to the agency signatory;

* any resolution ratifying agreements after the fact.
